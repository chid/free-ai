# Stay current on AI

AI moves fast enough that trying to follow every announcement becomes a second job. A small mix of independent analysis, primary sources, and technical discussion works better. Links checked 2026-10-07.

## A useful starter set

- **[The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/)** — weekly, readable coverage of AI news, research, and applications.
- **[Simon Willison's Weblog](https://simonwillison.net/)** — practical analysis of new models and AI tools from a software developer who tests what he writes about.
- **[Hugging Face Trending Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/trending)** — a quick way to see which research papers the community is discussing; read the abstract before relying on a summary.

Add a podcast or community that fits your interests, rather than subscribing to everything below.

## Newsletters

- **[The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/)** — a broad weekly overview, with accessible explanations of research and industry developments.
- **[Import AI](https://importai.substack.com/)** — Jack Clark's weekly newsletter, with more attention to research, policy, and what new results could mean.

## Blogs and primary sources

- **[Simon Willison's Weblog](https://simonwillison.net/)** — hands-on notes and analysis of models, coding tools, and practical AI engineering.
- Follow the labs' own updates when you want the original announcement or research: [OpenAI News](https://openai.com/news/), [Anthropic News](https://www.anthropic.com/news), and [Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/).
- **[Hugging Face Trending Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/trending)** — browse daily, weekly, or monthly research trends and follow links to papers and related models.

Lab announcements are primary sources for what a lab says it built; for performance or safety claims, look for independent evaluations and the underlying paper as well.

## Podcasts

- **[Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/podcast)** — AI engineering, agents, model infrastructure, and conversations with people building the systems.
- **[Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/archive)** — long-form interviews with AI researchers, founders, and other technical or policy voices.

## YouTube

- **[Two Minute Papers](https://www.youtube.com/@TwoMinutePapers)** — short, visual explanations of selected research papers. Useful for discovery; follow the paper link when a result matters to you.
- **[AI Explained](https://www.youtube.com/@aiexplained-official)** — independent explainers about new models and broader AI developments.

## Communities

- **[Hugging Face Discord](https://hf.co/join/discord)** — a large community around open models, datasets, and machine-learning projects.
- **[r/LocalLLaMA](https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/)** — especially useful for open-weight model releases, local inference, and hardware reports. It has a specific local-model focus, and individual claims still need checking.

## A low-noise routine

- Skim one weekly newsletter and save only stories tied to your interests.
- Follow release notes or research pages for the labs and projects you actually use; an RSS reader can collect them in one place.
- Check trending papers or a relevant community once a week. Read the source paper, release notes, or reproducible evaluation before treating a striking claim as settled.
- Try one development in a small, real task. Hands-on use is a good way to separate a useful change from a launch headline.

**If I had to keep only three:** The Batch for breadth, Simon Willison for practical analysis, and Hugging Face Trending Papers for research discovery. Add Latent Space for engineering depth or Import AI for research and policy context.
